Open with the real problem or decision, not a definition. What were you
actually trying to find out, and why did it matter enough to check?

## What I expected

State the assumption you were testing, in your own words, before showing
any result. If you were wrong, say so plainly when you get there.

## Setup

The minimum needed to make the result legible: what you measured, on what,
and how. Link to the full experiment rather than inlining every detail.
Only include a code snippet here if it explains a method or decision that
isn't obvious from the prose.

## What I found

Exact numbers. Separate the observation ("X measured 0.72") from what you
think it means; don't fuse the two into one sentence.

If there's a figure here, it should answer one clear question. Quantitative
charts use Observable Plot; exact values belong in an HTML table, not
crammed into chart labels. Dense secondary evidence (full matrices, full
per-item breakdowns) goes inside a `<details>` element, collapsed by
default.

## What didn't hold up

Every entry needs at least one failure, contradiction, or result you
couldn't resolve. This is usually the most useful section for a reader,
so don't compress it into one sentence.

## Implications

What this changes about how you'd actually build something, stated
narrowly for this setup, not "for AI" or "for search" in general.

## Limitations

Specific, not disclaimers. What would break this result: a different scale,
a different model, a different corpus, a different assumption you didn't
check.

## Conclusion

A few sentences. Don't re-summarize the whole entry. Say what you now
believe, plainly, and what you're still unsure about.
